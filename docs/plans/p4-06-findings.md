## Done (built 2026-11-15, awaiting demo)

| # | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| F1 | The light inside a cloud (`WeatherView.CloudLight`, the `_WxLight` the marched deck, the flat deck and the valley sea read) is the haze's own colour by day, mostly grey, instead of the blue sky ambient at 45%; the deck's darkest base is 70% of it, not 45%. Where the sun reaches a cloud, that light gives way (half of it at a sunlit top) so sunlit sides keep their form instead of clipping to white. |
| F2 | Inside and outside now share that brightness; the blend eases between "deck behind" and "deck in front" over 100 m across the base instead of switching; "seen from over it" counts from the deck's top, so the inside stays solid. |
| F3 | From above, the marched deck hides at most 45% (was 55–60%), the far film covers only the sky (the ground keeps its holes to 20 km), the shadow ceiling fades out over the ring's last 500 m instead of stopping in a wall, and the rays' upsample is depth-aware. |
| F4, F5 | At night and dusk the cloud light takes the moon's and the sky's light on the snow (0.55 × moon + 1.2 × sky ambient, greyed) when that's brighter than the horizon's navy. |
| F6 | The sky's deck runs on to 300 km, takes the horizon haze's colour from 30 km and thins out before the rays stop, so it never ends in a line; long far steps soften the cells' edges; snow curtains fade out by 30 km. |
| F7 | Cells lean with height and their carving changes with height (no upright walls); one tap toward the sun lights their sunward sides; long steps soften edges; the smoothing pass is centred (5×5, half-weight rim). The step cap near the camera wasn't needed on paper; temporal smoothing stays the fallback if the demo still shows combing. |
| F8 | The drift no longer jumps at its wrap (the turned tiles take the rest of the drift from `DriftCorrection`; the wrap is 315 km); the front never restarts mid-crossing, crosses in at most 80% of a face hour's real length, and now crosses the target (it was offset by the drift); the look eases evenly over the whole hour (no ease-then-hold), a pan eases over a second, and the year's turn no longer snaps it. |
