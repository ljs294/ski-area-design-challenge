Four art directions

You're right that Alpenglow read as a generic AI web dashboard: glass cards, pill badges, tiny spaced caps, thin line icons. These are four different directions instead, each built from things that belong to a ski resort rather than to a web app. They share one moment, planning a summit lift, over a capture of our actual Unity mountain. The status bar is back at the bottom in all four. D grows out of the MapLibre edition's own title screen and adds a title-screen frame. They're style frames for choosing a look, so the details are placeholders. Pick one, or name what you like in each.

A · Ops console the resort's control room

  • Hardware, not cards: machined plates, illuminated push-buttons for the tools, a knurled knob for game speed, and an annunciator panel that lights up for trouble.
  • The tool window is a screen with side keys, like a cockpit display. Break ground is the lit EXECUTE key.
  • Cockpit colour discipline: magenta for your plans (as pilots see a flight plan), cyan for what you selected, green for active, amber for caution.

B · Swiss signage information design, flat and bold

  • Signs, not panels: solid white sheets with a red top bar, big numerals, hard rules and plain sentence case. No glass, no pills, no shadows beyond the sheet.
  • Pictograms in the spirit of mid-century Winter Games signage, on square tiles in a black band. A station clock keeps the time.
  • On the mountain, labels are plates like piste signs, the plan is signal red, and warnings are yellow squares.

C · Trail-map print the resort's printed matter

  • Paper, ink and red pencil: tool windows are cards on the map table, plans are sketched in red pencil, and built things are inked.
  • A lift-ticket strip across the bottom holds the date and time, the tools as enamel pin badges, and the takings.
  • Breaking ground is a rubber stamp. Warnings are notes pinned to the map, and the problem is circled on the mountain.

D · Trailhead the MapLibre edition's title, carried into the game

  • The title screen keeps your signpost: the nameplate, and Continue, New Resort and My Resorts as trail-rated signs on cedar posts, standing on our Unity mountain at golden hour.
  • In the game, it becomes an instrument: warm graphite panels with hairline rules, Overpass for labels and Overpass Mono for figures. Every number has its units, and costs are broken down line by line.
  • Signage survives as accents: lift-number plates, trail-rating symbols and surveyor's orange for anything planned. The map carries thin, precise overlays with dimensions, not signs.
