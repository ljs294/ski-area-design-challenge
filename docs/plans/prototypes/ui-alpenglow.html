Alpenglow

Our own HUD, grown from the Subway Builder direction you preferred: calm glass and a simulator feel, but drawn as a ski trail map at night, with signage type, a conditions board, your centred tool dock, and one warm accent, alpenglow, for everything that's yours: your plans, the tool in use, the window in focus, and the button that builds. Two moments of the same day, then the kit they're made of. The earlier comparison is still there.

1 · Build: planning the summit lift

  • The tool dock sits bottom centre. Lifts opens a floating window with type, model, capacity and the lift's elevation profile, towers and all.
  • The plan tray above the dock collects plans from every tool. Break ground builds them all and shows the money left.
  • Plans are alpenglow: a dashed line between survey stakes, ghost towers, the length along the line, and one readout beside the stake with the keys you can use.
  • The trail map: lifts are fine lines with tower ticks and numbered plates; runs carry trail signs at their tops and names along their paths.

2 · Operate: a busy late morning

  • A view recolours the map: in Lift lines only the data has colour. Halos show the crowd at each base, tags give the wait, and guests say what they think.
  • The selected lift opens as a ticket: figures and riders by hour, its profile a tab away, and the actions on a perforated stub.
  • Alerts arrive as one card with a way to act. While a build tool is open they only count on the bell. The tray shrinks to a reminder when no build tool is open.

3 · The kit

  • Type: Barlow Condensed caps for titles and labels, like trail signage; Barlow Semi Condensed with tabular figures for everything else.
  • One accent: alpenglow means yours. Amber warns, red destroys, mint is good, ice marks the selected lift, and the difficulty colours stay trail-sign standard.

A still mockup: the mountain is generated, names and numbers are invented, and no other game's art or logos are used.
