Resort HUD Directions

One resort, one moment, two styles. It's midwinter at a made-up mountain; three lifts and five runs are open, and you're planning a lift to the summit with a new run off its top. Each frame shows the same things: the build tool with its blueprints, one selected lift, a data view and the status bar. These are still mockups, not playable; the blend you chose, a centred bar whose tools open floating windows, is in the build-flow prototype.

Leaning on Cities: Skylines II

  • Frosted glass throughout: a light glass toolbar of illustrated full-colour icons, a dark status band of rounded capsules, and panels that let the mountain show through.
  • Info views from the pale-blue button: an icon grid and a panel with a centred blue title, figures, a chart and a map legend, while the mountain pales so busy runs stand out.
  • One build window docked on the toolbar: lift cards, the tool's options, and the blueprints with their total and Build.
  • Placing in the world: a glowing ghost lift with handles and ghost towers, and one readout of length, rise, angle and cost beside the pointer.

Leaning on Subway Builder

  • Frosted glass panels that blur the map beneath, with centred titles, large pill tabs and a solid white pill for the current choice.
  • Round glass tool buttons bottom-left and a status bar of glass capsules, with figures in a monospaced face.
  • One Construction window: icon buttons, a stepper, the blueprint list and a blue Build button; blueprints glow yellow on the map with a live readout.
  • A schematic map where lifts read like transit lines, lift lines show as bubbles, and a legend capsule names the view that's on.
